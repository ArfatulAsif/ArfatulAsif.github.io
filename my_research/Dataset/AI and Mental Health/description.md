Dataset Description
The dataset consists of self-reported survey responses from 301 participants designed to measure the impact of digital habits and lifestyle factors on cognitive and behavioral well-being.

Demographics: 176 males (58.5%) and 125 females (41.5%), consisting predominantly of young adults and university students in Bangladesh (mostly aged 19 to 24).


Collection Method: Data was collected between June and August 2025 using a custom-built web application. The platform required all questions to be answered, resulting in a clean dataset with zero missing values or need for data imputation.


Features: The dataset comprises 55 custom questionnaire items, all scored on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree/Never to 5 = Strongly Agree/Always).



Predictor Variables (25 items): Tech Usage Habits (15 items measuring scrolling, notification checking, binge-watching) and Lifestyle/Psychosocial Factors (10 items measuring sleep, exercise, stress, anxiety/depression).


Outcome Variables (30 items): Three 10-item scales measuring Attention and Focus, Cognitive Load, and Behavioral Impulsivity.


Use Cases
Based on the study, this dataset is highly suited for:

Predictive Modeling: Training machine learning algorithms (like Random Forest, XGBoost, SVR, and KNN) to predict subclinical cognitive and behavioral outcomes based on everyday digital habits.


Feature Importance & Behavioral Analysis: Utilizing explainable AI techniques (like SHAP and permutation importance) to quantify whether technology usage or underlying lifestyle/emotional factors are the primary drivers of specific cognitive deficits.


Digital Well-being Screening: Developing accessible, self-report-based screening tools to identify young adults at risk of severe inattention, cognitive overload, or high impulsivity without requiring clinical diagnostic tests or hardware sensors.


Psychological Framework Validation: Testing theories related to continuous partial attention, cognitive load, and compensatory internet use in modern digital environments.


Availability
The data analyzed during this study are available from the me upon reasonable request. (Note: Only anonymized responses to the Likert-scale questionnaire will be provided; personal and demographic information such as email, gender, or age are excluded to protect participant privacy).
